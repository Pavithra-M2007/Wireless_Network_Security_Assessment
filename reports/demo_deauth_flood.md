##### [HIGH] DOS-001 — High Deauthentication Frame Count (Heuristic)

**Evidence:** `deauth_count = 20`

**Explanation:** Ten or more deauthentication or disassociation frames were associated with this BSSID during the capture period. This count meets the threshold commonly produced by automated deauthentication tools. This is a heuristic observation based on frame counts only — it does not prove that an attack occurred and does not identify an attacker or their intent.

**Impact:** If these frames originate from a forged source, affected clients may experience repeated disconnection events (denial of service). Forced reconnections can also create opportunities to capture WPA2 4-way handshake frames for offline analysis. Without Management Frame Protection there is no way to verify whether these frames are authentic or spoofed.

**Remediation:** Enable Management Frame Protection (IEEE 802.11w) to make deauthentication frames cryptographically authenticated and resistant to forgery. Deploy a Wireless Intrusion Prevention System (WIPS) for real-time detection of elevated deauthentication rates. Upgrade to WPA3-Personal, which mandates MFP for all clients.

##### [MEDIUM] AUTH-002 — Management Frame Protection Not Advertised (IEEE 802.11w)

**Evidence:** `mfp_capable = False`

**Explanation:** An RSN Information Element was observed but the RSN Capabilities field shows neither MFPC (Management Frame Protection Capable) nor MFPR (Required). All 802.11 management frames — including deauthentication and disassociation — are unauthenticated.

**Impact:** Deauthentication and disassociation frames can be forged trivially using a spoofed BSSID. An attacker may disconnect clients (denial of service), force reconnections to capture 4-way handshakes for offline analysis, or create conditions that assist an evil-twin attack — without breaking any encryption.

**Remediation:** Enable Management Frame Protection. For WPA2 networks, set MFP to Optional (MFPC=1, MFPR=0) initially to maintain backward compatibility with legacy clients, then set Required (MFPR=1) once all clients support IEEE 802.11w. Upgrading to WPA3 mandates MFP for all clients.
