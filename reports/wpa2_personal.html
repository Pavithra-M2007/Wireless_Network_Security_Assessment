HIGH AUTH-001 Wi-Fi Protected Setup (WPS) Advertised
Evidence: wps_present = True
Explanation: A WPS vendor-specific Information Element (OUI 00:50:F2, type 04) was observed in beacons from this AP, indicating that Wi-Fi Protected Setup is advertised as available to connecting clients.
Impact: WPS PIN mode is vulnerable to the Pixie Dust attack (offline brute-force of the enrollee nonce, often completing in under a minute) and to online brute-force tools. An attacker may recover the WPS PIN and through it the WPA2 passphrase without capturing any client traffic. Push-Button Configuration (PBC) avoids the PIN attack surface but exposes a two-minute open-enrollment window during which any nearby device can associate.
Remediation: Disable WPS entirely in the AP administration interface. Modern devices support direct passphrase entry and QR-code pairing without WPS. If WPS cannot be disabled, verify that the firmware has patched CVE-2011-5053 (Pixie Dust) and enforce rate-limiting on PIN attempts.
LOW AUTH-003 Management Frame Protection Optional, Not Required
Evidence: mfp_required = False
Explanation: The RSN Capabilities field shows MFPC=1 (capable) but MFPR=0 (not required). The AP accepts associations both with and without Management Frame Protection. Clients that do not negotiate MFP receive no protection for management frames.
Impact: Non-MFP clients on this network remain vulnerable to spoofed deauthentication and disassociation frames even while MFP-capable clients are protected. An attacker can selectively target legacy clients without affecting others.
Remediation: Set MFP to Required (MFPR=1) once all client devices support IEEE 802.11w. Legacy devices that do not support MFP should be retired or isolated to a separate VLAN or SSID. Upgrading to WPA3-Personal makes MFP mandatory for all clients.
